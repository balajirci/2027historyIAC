Corrections to use while studying

  • Menes and Narmer: identification is uncertain
  • Laozi: traditional attribution of the Daodejing
  • Zhang Heng: seismoscope, not a modern recording seismograph
  • Cai Lun: improvements to an existing papermaking tradition
  • Cape Agulhas is Africa’s southernmost point
  • Manet influenced the Impressionists but did not join their exhibitions
  • Pendleton Act initially covered only about 10% of federal positions
  • Boynton v. Virginia: interstate transportation facilities
  • Chinese imperial examination system developed beyond Han recruitment practices
  • Amenemhat III: Faiyum reclamation, not an established Nile–Red Sea canal claim
  • Dorian invasion: a debated interpretation
  • Xiongnu–Hun connections do not make the terms interchangeable

Other wording corrections: Vercingetorix was Gallic, not Gaelic; the Achilles heel tradition should not be attributed to the Iliad; the Pendleton Act did not immediately end all patronage. The China notes’ assertions about Han examinations and the Egypt canal claim are not carried forward. “Qin book burning and burying scholars” is presented as a traditional account with debated details.
